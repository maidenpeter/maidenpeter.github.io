Part II: Actuarial analysis Kenyan general insurers, reserve adequacy

Claims are reserved low and raised later: by 31% to 48% a year

Every quarter IRA prints each Kenyan insurer's claims movement: what was outstanding at the start, what was reported, paid, declined or closed, and what is outstanding at the end. Read from those balances, general insurers raised the estimates of claims already reported by 31.1% of the year's opening outstanding in 2023, 47.8% in 2024 and 33.4% in 2025, and most of them did it every year.

ToolsPython
Data

IRA's quarterly claims settlement statistics, 2023 to 2025: for every general insurer, liability and non-liability business, the amount of claims outstanding at the start of each quarter, reported, paid, declined, closed without payment and outstanding at the end, in KSh thousands. Read and checked in ira-kenya-data.

Method

A reserve adequacy test on case estimates. Over a year the closing balance is the opening one, plus the claims reported and the raises to their estimates, less what was paid, declined or closed, so the raises can be read from the balances. Each insurer's raise is set against what it had outstanding at the start of the year, checked against IRA's printed revisions, then compared insurer by insurer and year on year.

Step one

Read the raise from the balances, not from one column

IRA prints a column for revisions to claims already reported, but it cannot be added up as printed: one row in 2023 shows a revision of KSh 767bn, more than ten times the outstanding claims of the whole market. So the raise is read from the balances instead. For each insurer and year: what is outstanding at the end, plus everything paid, declined or closed during the year, less what was outstanding at the start and what was newly reported. What is left over is the amount by which estimates were raised after they were first set.

Set aside the one misprinted row and IRA's own column agrees with the balances, so it becomes a check rather than the source.

  • ✓

    The balances and the printed column agree. Raised, from the balances: 31.1%, 47.8% and 33.4% of the outstanding at the start of 2023, 2024 and 2025. IRA's printed revisions without the misprint: 30.4%, 44.9% and 35.0%.

  • ✓

    Only full years count. An insurer-year is used when all four quarters are printed for both kinds of business and it started the year with KSh 100m or more outstanding: 98 insurer-years from 35 insurers.

Step two

Every year, estimates went up by 31% to 48% of what was outstanding

At the start of 2025 the 33 insurers carried KSh 72.3bn of reported claims. During the year their estimates of reported claims were raised by 33.4% of that. In 2024 the raise was 47.8%, in 2023 31.1%.

Measured against new claims instead: for every KSh 100 of claims reported in a year, estimates were raised by KSh 27 to 42 later the same year.

Both kinds of business do it. Liability claims (claims by third parties, most of them injury from motor accidents) were raised by 28.2%, 36.3% and 43.7% in the three years; the rest (medical, own damage and property) by 33.4%, 55.9% and 26.5%.

Step three

Insurer by insurer, the same ones every year

The market figure is not a few insurers pulling the average. The middle insurer raised its estimates by 39.9%, 43.9% and 22.8% in the three years, and 26 of 33, 22 of 32 and 23 of 33 insurers raised them by more than 5%.

Of the 30 insurers in all three years, 18 raised by more than 5% in every year; only one lowered by more than 5% in every year. An insurer's rate in one year predicts its next: the rank correlation is 0.75 from 2023 to 2024 and 0.70 from 2024 to 2025. A pattern that holds insurer by insurer, year after year, is a reserving practice, not noise.

Step four

Trying to break it

  • ✓

    Is it IRA's misprint? No. The balances do not use the revisions column, and the column agrees with them once the one misprinted row is out.

  • ✓

    Is it insurers whose quarters do not join up? 13 insurer-years have a quarter that does not start where the one before ended. Leaving them out, the raise is higher: 41.4%, 53.5% and 48.9% on 85 insurer-years.

  • ✓

    Is it one kind of business? Liability 28.2% to 43.7%, the rest 26.5% to 55.9%: both, every year.

  • ✓

    Is it a few large insurers? The middle insurer raised by 22.8% to 43.9% a year, and most insurers raised by more than 5% every year.

  • !

    Does it mean the reserves are short? Not on its own. These tables carry case estimates only. A reserve also holds IBNR, and IBNR is where the expected raises belong.

So what

A case estimate is a first figure, not the reserve

In Kenya, claims are reserved low when they are reported and raised as they develop. That is safe only if the reserve also holds the raises still to come, as IBNR (strictly, incurred but not enough reported). An insurer that reserves at its case estimates with a thin IBNR is short, because the raises on the claims it holds have not happened yet. One that sizes its IBNR from its own rate of raises is not.

IRA's claims tables cannot say which insurer does which, because they do not show IBNR. The insurers' own IFRS 17 tables do. The one listed group whose table holds up with an estimate at every age, Liberty Kenya's in Kenya Reserve Check, shows its total estimates, IBNR included, 5.1% lower now than at the end of each year: there, the reserve set at the year end covered the raises that followed.

A first test for any other insurer: set its IBNR against a year of its own raises. Where the IBNR is smaller, the reserve rests on estimates the insurer has itself raised, year after year.

Limits

What this analysis cannot tell you

  • Case estimates only. IRA's claims tables show the claims reported and their estimates, not IBNR, so on their own they cannot say whether a reserve as a whole is enough.
  • The raises include claims reported during the year. They are set against the outstanding at the start of the year to give them a scale; they are not the run-off of that opening figure alone.
  • Three years. IRA has printed amounts only since the end of 2022, so 2023, 2024 and 2025 are the full years there are.
  • Returns as filed. Insurer-years whose quarters do not join up are kept in the main reading and left out in the check above. Insurers with less than KSh 100m outstanding, and the one misprinted row, are left out.
  • No insurer is named. The point is the practice across the market. Every insurer's figures are in IRA's tables and in the claims panel in ira-kenya-data.

Built with Python. tools/adequacy.py in this site's repository recomputes every figure from the claims panel, and the site's checks fail if a figure here differs.
