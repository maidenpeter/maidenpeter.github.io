All case studies Kenyan motor dealer group, anonymised

Revenue was being reported 61% higher than it was earned

Three system extracts from a Kenyan vehicle dealer group, 347,852 rows from January 2022 to August 2026. The brief was growth and margin. The first finding was that the revenue figure everyone was using counted the same money twice.

Rows347,852
PeriodJan 2022 to Aug 2026
Sources3 system extracts
ToolsPython, pandas, Excel
Data

Parts invoice lines, service job invoices and workshop consumption rows exported from the dealer's systems. The dealer, its branches, brand and counterparties are not named, and none of its data is published.

Method

Invoice-level matching across ledgers before any total; every unmatched invoice and the residual gap accounted for; bad rows flagged, not deleted; every figure produced in pandas and in Excel formulas and reconciled cell by cell.

Step one

Test the joins before adding the ledgers

The brief assumed three independent sources: a parts invoice ledger (185,699 lines), a service job ledger (30,787 rows) and a workshop consumption log (131,366 rows). Adding the two ledgers gave the revenue figure in use.

Before adding anything I matched invoice numbers across them. 28,666 of 30,779 job invoices, 93.1%, also appear as workshop sales on the parts ledger: the same parts, reaching the books by two routes. Each ledger is right on its own. Treating them as additive counted KSh 1.07bn twice.

Revenue was KSh 1.76bn, not KSh 2.83bn. Growth rates, branch shares and margin had all been measured against a base that did not exist.

  • ✓

    The 2,113 unmatched job invoices are labour only. None of them carries parts, so removing the overlap drops nothing.

  • ✓

    The KSh 27.3m residual between the ledgers is a price basis, not missing money. KSh 26.6m of it is 2022, when the job ledger carried parts at 1.25 times the parts ledger price; from 2023 the two agree to within rounding.

Step two

A few hundred bad rows decided the margin story

Margin is measured only on lines with a valid cost, from 2024 onward. The 2022 and 2023 lines are left unmeasured rather than estimated.

The later data had its own trap. 447 credit-sale lines in 2026 carried costs far above the normal level for the same items, about KSh 52m of excess cost. Left in, they put 2026 gross margin at 6.7% and the story becomes a collapse in profitability. Flagged and excluded, 2026 margin is 23.4%, in line with the 23.9% across all lines with valid cost.

A meeting held on the first number would have been about a crisis that was not happening.

Step three

Then answer the brief, on the corrected base

27%

a year compound revenue growth, 2022 to 2025.

32%

of job value came from accident repair, which was 3% of jobs: a commercial opportunity and a capacity risk in one number.

26%

of revenue came from 50 insurers as customers. Growth that leans on so few counterparties is growth with more concentration risk.

How it was checked

Every figure built twice

  • ✓

    5,189 of 5,189 headline cells match. Each figure was produced once in pandas and once as live Excel formulas, then compared cell by cell, so the client could open the workbook, change an assumption and watch the number move.

  • ✓

    Flags, never deletions. Every excluded row stays in the data with a flag column, so any exclusion can be reversed.

  • ✓

    Assumptions first. Every judgement call is one editable cell, stated before any result that depends on it.

Limits

What this analysis cannot tell you

  • Margin before 2024 is not reported. An estimate was possible, but not one that could be checked.
  • The 447 bad cost lines were flagged, not repaired. Their true cost is unknown, and imputing it would invent margin.
  • The 1.25 price ratio in 2022 is inferred from the data, not from documentation. It is consistent across the year and explains the gap, but the pricing policy behind it was not seen.
  • This is one dealer group in one market. The double-count pattern is common where a parts ledger and a job ledger sit side by side, but nothing here shows how common.
