Kenya's insurance market, rebuilt from the regulator's own returns

Where Kenyan insurers make money, and where they lose it

Every general insurer and reinsurer, every quarter from Q4 2016 to Q1 2026, extracted from the Insurance Regulatory Authority's statistics, checked against itself and turned into analysis an insurer's board or a reinsurer could use.

Insurers are named from IRA's public, unaudited returns, next to the method and caveats. A grade or a low loss ratio is a screening signal, not a finding of wrongdoing, and an insurer still writing business may have explanations the returns do not show.

Part I. The record

A return can add up on its own and still be wrong

Before any finding, the data: 42,246 checks, none unexplained, and 12 of 12 figures matching IRA's own PDF releases exactly.

IRA's tables say Intra-Africa Assurance had written KSh 4.32bn of premium by September 2024 and KSh 2.75bn by December. A running total cannot fall. Left in, that one return adds a KSh 2.4bn underwriting profit that never happened to the market's third quarter.

Intra-Africa is no rogue: it is one of five insurers that made an underwriting profit in every year from 2018 to 2025. Good companies file bad returns too, which is why every company is read across its quarters as well as checked for adding up.

The same checks found IRA's own printed totals wrong in three places, and tied 52 companies together through every rename and merger: UAP to Old Mutual General, AIG Kenya to NCBA Insurance, Jubilee General to Jubilee Allianz to Sanlam Allianz General.

The flag follows the company. On the reinsurer's scorecard, Intra-Africa's return quality score fell from 100 to 10 out of 100 in 2024, and its grade slipped from B to C.

Part II. The money

Insurers lose money on insurance, and motor is the hole

Underwriting profit is getting rarer: 19 of 35 insurers made one in 2016, 9 of 34 in 2025. The Loss Map has every class and insurer.

In every full year in the data, Kenya's general insurers lost money on the business they exist to do, and made it back on investments. In 2025 the gap was the widest yet: a KSh 7.0bn underwriting loss covered by KSh 21.3bn of investment income.

Motor is where the money goes. From 2018 to 2025 the market lost KSh 33.1bn on underwriting; motor alone lost KSh 52.6bn. Every other class together made KSh 19.6bn, KSh 13.1bn of it from workmen's compensation.

In 2025 that cushion slipped too: workmen's compensation made its first underwriting loss of the eight years, KSh -0.24bn.

The stakes are live. In September 2024 the Court of Appeal ruled that IRA can regulate premium rates, but struck down its 2009 motor guidelines because IRA had not shown that price setting was the right tool. A second attempt will need evidence of this kind.

Part III. The market

A crowded market, sorting itself out slowly

Who is growing, who is shrinking, and whether the market rewards pricing below cost.

Kenya had 34 general insurers in 2025 sharing KSh 227.2bn of premium. The Herfindahl-Hirschman index was 530, the same as 19 equal-sized insurers, and small insurers pay for it: those writing under KSh 3bn spent a median 34% of premium on management expenses in 2025, against 15% for those over KSh 10bn.

The market is sorting itself out, slowly. The ten insurers that lost share from 2018 to 2025 ran a median combined ratio of 112.0%; the 17 that gained ran 103.2%. Cheap growth is not being rewarded.

GA and Mayfair answer the market's usual excuse. Both grew about twice as fast as the market and still made an underwriting profit, at combined ratios of 89.6% and 88.7%. Tausi, a small insurer, ran 85.4%.

Market Structure has every insurer's share, concentration by class, and every entry and exit since 2016.

Part IV. The warning

A motor book that looks too good is more often a reserving question

Only three of the 40 insurers that wrote motor made money on it from 2018 to 2025. Two of them grew fast and priced properly. The third is a mirage.

The market's motor loss ratio ran between 65% and 81%. Ten insurers booked a year under 40%. Eight of those ten faced regulator action, negative equity or capital below IRA's floor at some point since 2018, against 7 of the other 28 motor insurers.

Trident booked 13% and 30% in 2021 and 2022, then stopped filing returns. It went into statutory management in March 2026. Invesco booked 11% in 2023, the year it was liquidated.

This is where an insurer's question becomes a reinsurer's. Not "is this good business?" but "can I believe these numbers?"

A low loss ratio is a warning, not proof. It can reflect a good book, heavy reinsurance or thin reserves. Tausi booked three low years with no trouble at all, and Definite's 2025 figure is its first year, when claims lag premium.

Part V. The verdict

The warning was in the data a year before the regulator acted

Every insurer graded the way a reinsurance underwriter screens a cedant. The Cedant Scorecard has every insurer and year.

A reinsurer asks four questions of an insurer before taking its business: does it make money on underwriting, can its balance sheet stand behind the business, what kind of book is it, and can its numbers be trusted. The scorecard answers them for every insurer, every year, from public data alone.

Five insurers have been placed under statutory management or liquidation since 2022. Resolution was graded D in every year scored. Xplico was 29th of 33 and Invesco 33rd of 33, graded E. Three of the five sat in the bottom fifth a year before the regulator acted.

The other two, Trident and Corporate, fell out of the table because they stopped filing complete returns. Silence is a signal: a reinsurer should treat a missing return as a warning in its own right.

Britam General carried the largest share of the market's motor losses, yet climbed from 15th in 2019 to 2nd in 2025 as its results recovered and its equity rose from KSh 2.5bn to KSh 8.4bn. The loss map shows its past; the scorecard shows its present.

Five cases is a small sample, and the test cannot show how many low-graded insurers carried on without trouble. Change the weights and the ranking barely moves: it correlates at 0.97 to 0.99 with the base case in every year.

Epilogue

Who screens the screeners?

The local reinsurers made an underwriting profit in six of the nine years, then lost KSh 0.94bn in 2025. Kenya Reinsurance Corporation, the largest by premium, lost KSh 1.58bn on its own, more than the whole group.

Next, the series moves from what insurers earn to whether they pay. IRA's claims settlement statistics from 2017 to 2025 are already collected. Resolution was placed under statutory management over unsettled claims; that is the human cost of everything above.

OpenReinsurance flows

Kenya Reinsurance Flows

Kenyan general insurers passed KSh 66.6bn of premium to reinsurers in 2025, 29.3% of what they wrote: 3.2% of motor, 99.3% of aviation. At most 49% of it could have gone to the five reinsurers that file IRA returns. The local reinsurers' net commission rose from 23.2% of earned premium in 2018 to 32.0% in 2025.

Data
Gross and net premium by class for every general insurer, and the local reinsurers' returns, full years 2018 to 2025.
Method
Cession and retention by class, insurer and size, reconciled to each insurer's outward reinsurance; the local reinsurers' results by class; retention against the Cedant Scorecard.
Tools
Python, pandas and numpy; class totals tested against IRA's PDF releases with pytest

Method

From the regulator's files to a finding

A return can add up on its own and still be wrong, so every company is also read across its quarters. Errors in the official tables are documented, not hidden: every one of the 97 failed checks traces to a known IRA publishing error.

Collect165 files

Every IRA statistical publication online from 2008, catalogued with source links and SHA-256 hashes.

Extract233,685 rows

Revenue accounts and class tables from workbooks whose layouts and company names shift from year to year.

Validate42,246 checks

Accounting identities, class totals, IRA's printed totals and a cross-check against the PDF releases.

Flag12 returns

Returns that add up but cannot be right, such as year-to-date premium that falls, kept out of quarterly totals.

Analyse5 pages

Profitability, market structure, reinsurance flows, a reinsurer's scorecard and an explorer, each rebuilt by one command.

Built in Python, checked at every step

Every published number is rebuilt from the raw files, and the raw files are listed with their IRA source links and hashes. The charts on this page are plain HTML and CSS, and every one has a table of its numbers.
